Cortical phosphene vision simulator

Interactive simulation of camera-based visual encoding for a hypothetical penetrating-electrode array in human primary visual cortex (V1).
What is being simulated: each electrode samples a camera-derived activation map and produces a localized white phosphene. Electrode density follows cortical magnification, while phosphene size reflects estimated electrical current spread in V1. Each electrode has a stable, mildly elliptical phosphene shape and orientation; central phosphenes tend to have more defined edges, while peripheral phosphenes become softer and cloudier. Encoding modes: Sobel emphasizes local luminance boundaries; Luminance encodes image intensity directly; Luminance + Sobel combines surface brightness with boundary information. This is a research visualization, not a patient-specific prediction.

Model and limitations

The model uses mildly elliptical generalized-Gaussian phosphenes with phenomenological eccentricity-dependent edge softness and linear summation across electrodes. The shape distribution is not calibrated to an individual patient. It does not model patient-specific retinotopy, cortical folding, eye movements, nonlinear interactions between nearby electrodes, stimulation safety, or the full temporal structure of electrical pulse trains. Adaptation is a simple phenomenological approximation.
